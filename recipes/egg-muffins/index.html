Egg Muffins

Grab-and-go egg muffins with cottage cheese blended into the eggs — that's the trick that keeps them fluffy instead of rubbery, even reheated.

By FitLifeChef · Published Sunday, November 29, 2026 · Nutrition calculated per serving from the ingredients below — method and source under the recipe card.

Ingredients

Metric first, US measures in brackets. For 4 servings.

  • 8 eggs
  • 200g (7 oz) cottage cheese
  • 100g (3.5 oz) spinach, wilted and squeezed dry
  • 1 bell pepper, 150g (5.3 oz), finely diced
  • 80g (2.8 oz) feta or cheddar, crumbled
  • 4 spring onions, sliced
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 180°C (350°F). Oil a 12-hole muffin tin well — eggs stick like they mean it.
  2. Blend the eggs and cottage cheese until smooth, then stir in everything else.
  3. Divide between the holes, filling each about three-quarters full.
  4. Bake 16–18 minutes until just set in the middle with a slight wobble.
  5. Cool 5 minutes in the tin, then loosen with a knife. Serve 3 per portion, warm or cold.

FitLifeChef tip

Squeeze the wilted spinach properly dry in a clean tea towel. Wet spinach is the number one cause of soggy, sunken egg muffins.

Swaps

Keep the egg-and-cottage-cheese base and swap freely: ham and peas, sun-dried tomato and basil, or mushroom and gruyère are all proven.

Storage

Leftovers keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days unless the FAQ below says otherwise. Reheat until piping hot throughout.

Questions

How long do they keep?

4 days in the fridge, 3 months in the freezer. Reheat from chilled for 40 seconds in the microwave — any longer and eggs turn rubbery.

What other fillings work?

Keep the egg-and-cottage-cheese base and swap freely: ham and peas, sun-dried tomato and basil, or mushroom and gruyère are all proven.
